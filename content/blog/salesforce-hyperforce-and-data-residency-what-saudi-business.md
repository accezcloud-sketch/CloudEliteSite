## 1. Understanding Salesforce Hyperforce: A New Foundation for Customer 360

### The Problem
For years, businesses globally, including those in Saudi Arabia, have relied on Salesforce's robust CRM capabilities. However, a persistent concern has been the perceived lack of direct control over the physical location of their data, which historically resided in Salesforce-managed data centers. This created potential complexities around meeting specific data residency mandates and regulatory requirements, particularly for industries with stringent data governance policies.

### Why It Happens
Salesforce's original architecture was designed for global scalability and flexibility, utilizing a centralized model for its data centers. While this model ensured high availability and performance, it meant that data for customers in different regions might not necessarily be stored within those specific geographical boundaries, leading to compliance challenges for some organizations.

These regulations aim to ensure that national data remains within the country's borders where possible, or is subject to stringent cross-border transfer protocols, giving governments oversight and control over data relevant to their jurisdiction.

### The Problem
The advent of Hyperforce fundamentally alters the data residency conversation for Salesforce users.
